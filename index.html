    Sensor notes

    MODIS has pixels of about 1 km. VIIRS has pixels of about 375 m, so it notices smaller and cooler fires and reports far more detections for the same landscape. The two sensors also pass over at different times of day. Raw counts therefore cannot be compared directly.

    How we make the sensors comparable

    • We do not convert pixels from one sensor to the other.
    • Fire cell-days count each 0.25° map cell once per day if it has at least one detection. This reduces the effect of pixel size and repeated passes.
    • Total fire radiative power (FRP) adds up the heat output in megawatts. A value below 1 in the table means VIIRS reports less total power than MODIS, because each of its small pixels carries less.
    • The harmonized view divides each sensor by its own average and then averages the two. A value of 1 is an average day for that sensor.

    Limits to keep in mind

    • A satellite sees a place only a few times a day. A day with no detection recorded does not mean there was no fire.
    • Clouds and thick smoke can hide fires.
    • Hotspots can come from burning other than wildfire, such as planned or agricultural burns. We keep only the vegetation-fire type and nominal or high confidence detections.
    • The overlap ratios are estimates, not exact conversions.
    • This tool shows history only. It does not predict fires and does not replace official alerts.
